Halyard revenue desk

AI agents that run the revenue side of a small distributor: take orders from email, bill customers, pay suppliers, and reconcile the books. A controller keeps only the decisions.

PROMPTHow this relates to the Hyphenate track: "The Hyphenated Enterprise: Autonomous Systems for Finance"

The ask: pick a company and a few finance seats that work closely together, then build the autonomous system you would hire to fill them, plus the company it works in. The system should pick up work on its own, be explainable and accountable, and respect limits.

Our corner: Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable and Cash, plus a reconciliation seat. The company is Halyard Marine Supply, a small distributor. Seats: order entry, customer billing, cash application, vendor bills and payments, reconciler. The controller keeps the decisions.

The hard parts the prompt names, and where we meet them:

  • Sources that disagree. S03: the email says $4.00 and the PDF says $4.40, and the PDF governs with the reason recorded. S07: a supplier overbills $13 a unit and the 3-way match flags it. Reconcile compares our ledger to Odoo's trial balance and to the bank.
  • Time. Phase 1 of the scenarios teaches a desk something and phase 2 tests it. S04 is a contract customer quoted at list price, the controller rejects it, orders rewrites its workflow, and S08 (the same customer later) is caught with no controller. Short-pays and receipts also arrive after the invoice. Gap: we don't simulate month-end close or period locking.
  • Working with others. Drafts wait for a controller approval, and a rejection triggers revision. Gap: in the benchmark the controller is a rule-based stand-in reading the answer key; a person can take that seat through a review card.
  • Limits. A role may write only its own event kinds, nobody approves what they drafted, and vendors are paid only to the bank account on file in Odoo. Gap: identities are asserted, not authenticated.
  • Explainable and accountable. Every event names actor, role, workflow version and source down to the attachment page, in an append-only hash-chained ledger. trace explains any document.

The four judging questions:

  • Real enough? Real Odoo 18 documents and journal entries, a real Increase sandbox bank, PDFs generated and read for real. The messiness is planted: ten scenarios with an answer key, scored by eval.score, with real and simulated events counted separately.
  • Would you hire it? Limited roles, a clean record of its work, and it asks before anything posts or pays. It also refuses the injected "pay this account" email (S09).
  • Something new? See the next green box.
  • The moment. It catches its own blind spot: after a rejection, it fixes its own process and then catches the repeat unaided.

Submission list: one line on seats, proof of what is real versus simulated, and code are covered. The demo video that opens on the moment and the live demo still have to be made.

Intake

Reads emails and PDFs, classifies them, extracts the quote, and flags duplicates, conflicts and suspicious instructions.

Orders

Turns a quote into a sales order and purchase orders in Odoo, using contract prices and the right suppliers.

Billing

Issues customer invoices and checks vendor bills with a 3-way match (order, receipt, bill).

Cash

Applies customer payments and pays vendors, only after approval and only to the bank account on file.

Reconcile

Compares our ledger to Odoo's trial balance and to the bank, and pins any mistake on the desk that made it.

Architect

Runs once up front. Studies Odoo, the bank and the inbox, writes the company profile and policy, and builds each desk's workflow.

The desk diagnoses why its own workflow let the mistake through, edits a proposed copy, and validates it. A revision goes live only with a controller approval. It can add controls but never remove them or change existing thresholds.

NOVELWhat is new here

  • Workflows that rewrite themselves under control. When a desk is overruled, it diagnoses why its own state machine let the mistake through and proposes a fix. The fix needs a controller approval, can add controls but never remove them, and the old version is archived. Scenarios S04 and S08 show it: the same customer is caught with no controller needed after the revision.
  • Gates the agent cannot talk its way past. A transition fires only if the agent says yes and a Python check reads the ledger and agrees. Posting to Odoo and sending money are blocked in code until an approval exists.
  • One hash-chained ledger with sources down to the attachment page. trace explains any document back to the email that caused it.
  • Built-in adversarial tests. Ten planted scenarios with an answer key, including a prompt-injection email and a vendor who overbills, scored automatically.
  • An architect that surveys the company first and compiles one validated workflow per desk, rather than shipping a fixed pipeline.

Answering "why did it do that?"

Every event in the ledger records who acted, in what role, under which workflow version, and from which sources (down to the email attachment page). python -m ledgerdb trace <entity> walks a document from the quote through the order, invoice and payments back to the mail and bank.

NOVELCompare and contrast in one line

Alike: everyone is automating the AP and AR back office with LLMs and keeping a human on approvals. Different: the commercial tools sell the automation, the connectors sell the access, and ours treats the controls and the agents' own workflow changes as the product. In the sources I found, none described agents revising their own workflows after a rejection. That may be my search, not proof it does not exist.

Known limits

Identities are asserted, not authenticated, so the controls stop mistakes but not an agent that lies about who it is. Odoo uses one shared admin login. Money is handled as floats rounded to cents. The benchmark controller is rule-based. Full list in README.md.
